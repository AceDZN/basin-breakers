# Freeform rescue: bounded visual review

## Campaign extension review

After winning the prototype, the human explicitly requested further levels. The earlier one-map hold below is historical; a 24-level freeform campaign is now being implemented and verified. [Generated target 08](mockups/08-campaign-level2-opening.png) was generated, corrected and inspected by the art owner and lead **before** dependent campaign renderer work. It establishes a western source, eastern/downstream homes, an actual limestone ridge and a compact `שלב 2 מתוך 24` HUD. The first draft incorrectly showed discharge during planning; the selected correction closes the gate and leaves the valley dry. The record and exact prompts are in [the mockup README](mockups/README.md). `השלב הבא` belongs to an actual win, so an active next button is intentionally absent from the unbuilt target.

The campaign uses `src/freeform/campaign-scene.ts`; the playable prototype's `scene.ts` was left unchanged. The scene reads each model's source, homes, actual bed, editable mask, permeability and actual coast sink positions. Camera fitting includes the current legal soil extent, source structure and house bounds. Reservoir level represents the fraction of all declared pulse water still held. The automatic gate opens only while a ready pulse still contains water, closes between delayed pulses and closes once empty. The future pulse forecast does not become early discharge. Stone features remain the authoritative terrain surface; no taller decorative boulder is used to invent a physical barrier. Levee bodies still match their actual occupied cells and crest, with stock/inventory owned by the model/UI.

Actual browser inspection at 1440×900 and 390×844 covered level 2, wetland level 9 and combined level 24. The western/eastern source and home movement is plainly visible, two-levee stock and stage progress agree with the model, and wetland ground is green only where actual `state.perm` is positive. All 24 maps were selected through the real map menu at 390×844: exactly one canvas remained after every selection, and the source/home projected centers stayed within the visible usable frame. Manually inspected important-object bounds on 2/9/24 were not clipped. This is framing/component evidence, not evidence that every level is solved or enjoyable.

Repeated replacements produced no browser/GLSL errors and no WebGL context-limit warnings. A separate delayed-asset race introduced a 450ms local GLB response delay, switched 1→2→3 through the UI, and ended with one canvas, level 3 and no errors. Each scene owns cloned procedural GPU geometry/materials; `dispose()` removes the resize listener, releases geometry, materials, textures, instance buffers, shadow resources and renderer context. A late GLB completion is guarded and releases its unused resources. Main owns the RAF loop. This test does not measure exact VRAM usage or prove absence of every possible browser memory leak.

TypeScript checking passes. The required develop-web-game action client was run on `campaign.html`; its opening screenshot was opened and inspected, with no console-error artifact. Temporary art evidence is in `work/art/campaign/` (2/9 desktop and phone, 24 phone/desktop, all-map `switch-summary.json`) and `work/art/campaign-skill-final/`. Lead/QA retain the authoritative gameplay evidence separately.

**Art verdict:** campaign variation is real and the main objects remain readable, but target 08's stronger irregular ridge and natural catchment silhouette are not matched. The actual ridge still reads as a modest stony raised patch; wetland edges expose the coarse physical cell mask. The smooth ochre floor, straight pale upper band, repeated outer blocks and conspicuous interior caustic cells remain material/composition gaps. No photoreal parity, campaign solvability, player enjoyment or learning claim follows from this renderer review. Further physical silhouette work must change the real bed and be revalidated, rather than hide the discrepancy with decorative obstacles. No additional Tripo processing or model credits were used.

## Final bounded renderer changes

The final sea pass removes the cell-caustic material from the external sea, adds very low-contrast continuous moving swells/normal ripples and a narrow lapping line along the exterior beach, and retains darkening with distance. Initial GPU inspection caught overly bright swell streaks; their brightness was reduced before the final freeze. No source volume, bed height, edit mask, coast sink, flow rate or win condition changed. The sea is clipped from underneath the true model rectangle so it cannot visually fill a deep excavation during planning.

Coast strips retain the last actual received-driven discharge on paused, failed and won states, making a frozen consequence inspectable. They do not create water in the model or declare a whole coast successful. Outer limestone props stay on fixed shoulders; visible levee bodies match their actual occupied cells and crest. Preview and stable picking use separate meshes so the apparent trench does not shift its own input surface.

TypeScript checking passes. The required action-client opening smoke runs without a console-error artifact and its resulting screenshot is visually inspected. Separate gameplay/control regression is still required after recovery changes; this document does not replace it.

## Verdict and next gate

This renderer is a clearer **freeform physical prototype** than the rejected marked-cut interaction. It is not at visual-target parity, and no claim of human acceptance, fun or learning follows from its screenshots or numerical witnesses. Preserve working undo/fill/retry and verify the user's stuck sequence first. Then ask the human to invent a plan on this one map before campaign expansion. If the loop is accepted, the meaningful art work is reshaping the authoritative terrain into an irregular catchment, strengthening mineral/soil contrast, and making the water's moving front more coherent—without hiding geometry, adding an answer route, or spending more asset credits.
